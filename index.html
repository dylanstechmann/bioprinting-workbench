        INDEPENDENT ARITHMETIC
        Relative doublings, not organ yield
        Inputs below are synthetic assumptions, not recommendations or literature presets. Control bounds are software limits, not biological ranges.
        Genome stability ≠ division speed
        DNA replication, DNA damage, mutation, culture selection, senescence and residual pluripotent cells are separate questions. This app measures none of them and calculates no cancer probability.
        Would chemicals be sprinkled over cells for DNA repair?
        Not as a generic repair coating. Halliwell et al. studied nucleosides added to PSC culture medium and reported reduced replication stress and genome-damage markers; the published formulation and culture conditions are restated below as a cited research-reference card. That is not a universal repair treatment or evidence that existing mutations, mutant clones or cancer risk were eliminated, and the same intervention class produced adverse genome-instability findings in mouse ES cells (see the method-evidence panel).

        HYPOTHETICAL LONGEVITY THEORY · SIMULATION ONLY
        How close is printed-organ replacement to eternal youth? (theory, not measurement)
        Serious theory, honestly bounded: printed organs could at best address deaths from single-organ failure; aging itself is systemic and multi-hallmark, and the only in-vivo amelioration evidence reviewed is mouse reprogramming and mouse parabiosis — no human result, no eternal-youth path. The whole-body-replacement lever is shown but never selectable. Theory write-up.
        
    
        HYPOTHETICAL APPLICATIONS LEDGER · SIMULATION ONLY
        What could people actually use? (measured, preclinical, theory)
        Cosmetic, functional and research applications with their real status: printed ears and hair-bearing skin exist in animal models, human-scale shaped constructs have been printed with microchannels against the 100–200 μm diffusion limit, and printed human adipose cell models already serve preliminary drug testing. No clinical bioprinting result is claimed. Applications write-up · Why organs are not chips.
        
    
        HYPOTHETICAL BREAST SOFT-TISSUE COMPARISON · SIMULATION ONLY
        Printed breast tissue versus silicone implants: the honest ledger (theory, not measurement)
        Augmentation and reconstruction are where the wish meets the evidence. Silicone implants carry a measured adverse record (revision procedures and BIA-ALCL with textured implants); autologous fat grafting retains only part of the grafted volume; flaps are vascularized but major surgery; printed adipose tissue exists only in animals and in-vitro models. The unregulated-injectable shortcut is shown and never selectable. Nothing here recommends anything. Comparison write-up.

        IDEAS DESK, MADE INTERACTIVE · FETCHED OPEN-ACCESS EVIDENCE
        The genome-backup-and-restore wish: which steps are real?
        Record the original genome to disk, keep original cells banked, fetch the stored DNA periodically to rewrite fast-dividing cells, maybe edit toward differentiation. Each step is selectable and gets a computed verdict from four freshly fetched open-access sources: sequencing-to-storage and cell banking are established practice; the periodic whole-genome write-back and edit-steered differentiation are theory — CRISPR recording writes bits of cell history into barcodes, not stored genomes back into chromatin, and somatic mosaicism means there is no single original genome to restore. Method and sources.
          Verdicts describe research maturity, never safety, feasibility for any person or animal, or an approval. Recording into DNA exists as event barcodes; whole-genome write-back does not exist in any reviewed source.

          From tissue models toward whole organs?
          A liver assembloid is not an entire manufactured liver. More elapsed time does not itself solve architecture, supply, drainage, identity or stability. No whole-organ fabrication duration is established here. Scope and evidence.

          Model, assets & missing biology
          All geometry is original procedural artwork, inspired by generic enclosed printers, culture equipment and perfusion setups. Three.js 0.187.0 (MIT) is bundled locally. No patient data, network APIs, analytics or vendor models are used. The oxygen slab is a separate analytical teaching model, not coupled to the construct. Independent normalized-box oxygen and resistance-network models do not establish tissue transport or fluid dynamics. Missing: coupled transport, cell mechanics, endothelial function, immune response, contamination, scaffold degradation, host anastomosis and healing. The culture, construct and recipient views use different illustrative scales. The Treedome theme is original fan-inspired artwork referencing the fictional Sandy Cheeks setting from SpongeBob SquarePants (Nickelodeon); unofficial and unaffiliated, using no show assets.
