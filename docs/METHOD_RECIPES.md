## Nucleoside supplementation in human PSC culture — published method reference (Halliwell et al. 2020)

What the reviewed study reported: human PSC culture media were supplemented with a defined nucleoside formulation, with assays after 72 h. Parameters are transcribed from the open-access Experimental Procedures with locators; this is a cited description of published research, not a recommendation or treatment instruction.

- Method evidence row: `halliwell-nucleosides` (improved-endpoint; timeline evidence: reported-study-endpoint)
- Applies to cell systems: human-ipsc, human-esc
- Primary source: Nucleosides Rescue Replication-Mediated Genome Instability of Human Pluripotent Stem Cells (2020), DOI 10.1016/j.stemcr.2020.04.004 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7355123/

**Observed endpoints.** Increased replication-fork speed and longer newly synthesized DNA fibers · Reduced replication origin density · Reduced γH2AX damage markers and mitotic aberrations · Improved survival, clonogenicity and population growth in the studied lines (68% to 79% of tracked cells entered mitosis; 59% to 91% formed colonies)

**Adverse evidence beside this card.**
- Forced fork acceleration in mouse ES cells caused miscoordination between completion of DNA replication and cell-cycle progression, increasing ultra-fine bridges from about 20% to about 40% of cells and micronuclei; γH2AX increased from 6.1% to 13.1%. (Embryonic stem cells maintain high origin activity and slow forks to coordinate replication with cell cycle progression 2024, Abstract; Results and discussion: Replication fork acceleration causes miscoordination between DNA replication and cell cycle progression in ES cells; Figures 3E and 4C–G)
- In mouse ES cells, exogenous ribonucleoside supplementation accelerated replication-fork speed throughout S phase and reduced active origin density while S-phase duration stayed similar and the overall growth rate was not affected. (Embryonic stem cells maintain high origin activity and slow forks to coordinate replication with cell cycle progression 2024, Results and discussion: fork acceleration and origin activation sections; Figures 3 and 4)

**Limits.** Describes the studied lines (MIFF1, TC113, MShef11) under the reported media conditions; outcomes are study-specific. Reduced damage markers and improved growth in these cultures do not remove existing mutations or mutant clones and establish no transplant readiness. The same intervention class produced adverse genome-instability findings in mouse ES cells (linked adverse evidence); species, cell state and concentration differ. One tested human iPS line (PChiPS771) showed no fork-asymmetry reduction under its tested medium condition (Kurashima et al., Figure EV3G). Expansion endpoints in the source do not establish a whole-organ production timeline.

## Serum-free/xeno-free expansion of human BM-MSCs — published method reference (Bhat et al. 2021)

What the reviewed study reported: human bone-marrow MSCs were expanded from a multi-donor pool across compared low-serum, serum-free and xeno-free media at two seeding densities, with doubling kinetics estimated from P3 to P5. **Adverse evidence beside this card.**
- Forced fork acceleration in mouse ES cells caused miscoordination between completion of DNA replication and cell-cycle progression, increasing ultra-fine bridges from about 20% to about 40% of cells and micronuclei; γH2AX increased from 6.1% to 13.1%. The same commercial nucleoside stock family is referenced by the human recipe, used here at a different dilution; species, cell state and conditions differ.
